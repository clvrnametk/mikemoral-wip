What should Disney+ become?

The situation

A few years into building Disney+, the organization had no shortage of momentum. Executive priorities were multiplying. The Hulu integration was underway. Dozens of initiatives were competing for investment, each evaluated primarily on projected business value.

What didn't exist was a unified experience point of view connecting any of it.

The risk was not a lack of ideas. The risk was that we would keep shipping a collection of projects rather than building a coherent product. Everyone was busy. Alignment was getting harder. And the most important question nobody had time to answer was the simplest one: what should Disney+ become?

The bet

The standard move is a vision deck. A small senior group goes offsite, produces something beautiful, and presents it to an organization that had no hand in making it. Those decks get applauded and then ignored, because a vision people didn't build is a vision people don't execute.

We bet the other way. We brought together roughly sixty leaders and makers across Design, Product, Engineering, and Research and gave them one job: define a cohesive future direction for the product. Not concepts. Not inspiration. A direction the organization could actually execute against.

The hard part was never generating the ideas. The hard part was creating enough clarity that hundreds of people could move in the same direction without being told what to do at every step.

What changed

The work was reviewed at the highest levels of leadership and became part of long-term product planning. It shaped multiple years of product investment. Several major initiatives on the platform today trace directly or indirectly back to it.

The most important change was harder to see on a roadmap. The organization shifted from reacting to executive requests toward executing against a coherent experience vision. Investments started connecting to each other. Teams could explain not just what they were building, but why it belonged.

What persists

Years later, the direction is still shaping the platform. That is the test I hold this kind of work to. A good idea helps a team. A clear direction helps an organization, and it keeps helping long after the original work is done.

The lesson was not that vision matters. Everyone knows vision matters.

The lesson is that clarity scales.
